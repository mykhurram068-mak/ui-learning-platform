👩 BMI for Women

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read · Includes healthy range chart

To calculate BMI for a woman, use the same formula as for men: divide weight in kilograms by height in meters squared. For example, a woman who weighs 60 kg and is 1.65 m tall has a BMI of 22.0 — in the healthy range (18.5–24.9).

While the formula is identical, BMI interpretation for women involves different considerations — from essential fat requirements to hormonal changes across life stages. This guide covers how to calculate BMI, healthy ranges for women, and what makes BMI different for women.

The BMI Formula for Women

Metric
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

The formula is the same for men and women. The difference lies in how the result is interpreted — because healthy body composition differs between the sexes.

Example: BMI Calculation for a Woman

A woman weighs 62 kg and is 1.68 m tall. What is her BMI?

Worked Example
Step 1: Square the height — 1.68 × 1.68 = 2.8224
Step 2: Divide weight by squared height — 62 ÷ 2.8224 = 21.97
→ BMI ≈ 22.0 (healthy range)

What Makes BMI Different for Women?

1. Women have more essential body fat

Women naturally carry 10–13% essential fat, compared to 2–5% for men. This is required for hormonal function, menstrual cycles, and reproductive health. As a result, women can have a healthy BMI but a higher body fat percentage than men at the same number.

2. Fat distribution is different

Women typically carry more subcutaneous fat on the hips, thighs, and buttocks — a "pear" shape. Men more often carry visceral fat around the abdomen — an "apple" shape. Visceral fat is more strongly linked to cardiovascular and metabolic risk, which is one reason BMI alone doesn't capture the full picture.

3. Hormonal changes affect BMI

Pregnancy, breastfeeding, perimenopause, and menopause all change body composition. Women naturally gain fat and lose muscle during menopause, even if their BMI stays the same. This is why healthy BMI ranges can look slightly different at different life stages.

4. Athletes and muscular women

Female athletes and women who strength train often show higher BMI due to muscle mass rather than fat. A BMI of 26 in a muscular woman can reflect excellent health, while a BMI of 22 in an inactive woman can mask elevated body fat.

While the formula doesn't change, healthy body composition does. Here's a rough guide to how BMI interpretation can shift across life stages:

Why ranges shift with age: Muscle mass declines naturally after 30 — and more rapidly after menopause. A slightly higher BMI in older women often reflects bone density and healthy weight reserves, which protect against falls and fractures.

BMI During Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

BMI is not reliable during pregnancy or in the months of breastfeeding. Healthcare providers use pre-pregnancy BMI to recommend healthy weight gain ranges, but the standard categories don't apply while pregnant.

Pre-pregnancy BMI guidelines for weight gain:
• Underweight (BMI <18.5): 12.5–18 kg (28–40 lb)
• Normal (BMI 18.5–24.9): 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb)
• Overweight (BMI 25–29.9): 7–11.5 kg (15–25 lb)
• Obese (BMI 30+): 5–9 kg (11–20 lb)

When BMI Is Misleading for Women

  • Muscular women — BMI overestimates body fat in athletes and strength trainers
  • Older women — BMI may miss age-related muscle loss and bone density changes
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women — BMI does not apply
  • Women of South Asian, Chinese, or other Asian descent — WHO recommends lower thresholds: healthy 18.5–22.9, overweight 23.0–27.4, obese 27.5+
  • Women with PCOS or thyroid conditions — BMI may not reflect metabolic health accurately

Better Measurements to Pair with BMI

For a fuller picture of health, combine BMI with:

  • Waist circumference — healthy <88 cm (35 in) for women
  • Waist-to-hip ratio — healthy <0.85 for women
  • Body fat percentage — healthy 20–32% for adult women (varies by age)
  • How clothes fit and how you feel — a practical, underrated indicator

Practical takeaway: BMI is a starting point, not a verdict. If your BMI is in the healthy range but you carry significant abdominal fat, your health risk may still be elevated. If your BMI is slightly high but you exercise and have good muscle mass, you may be healthier than the number suggests.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy BMI for a woman?

For most adult women, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered healthy. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is obese.

How do I calculate BMI for a woman?

The BMI formula is the same for women and men. Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)².

Does BMI differ for women by age?

The BMI formula is the same at any adult age, but healthy body composition changes with age. After menopause, women naturally lose muscle and gain fat, so a BMI at the higher end of normal may carry more risk. Women over 65 may benefit from a slightly higher BMI (23–27) for bone health.

Is BMI accurate during pregnancy?

No. BMI is not a reliable measure during pregnancy. Healthcare providers use pre-pregnancy BMI to recommend weight gain ranges, but the general BMI chart does not apply while pregnant.

Why does BMI matter differently for women?

Women naturally carry more essential fat than men for hormonal and reproductive function. Body fat distribution also differs — women tend to carry more subcutaneous fat on hips and thighs. Same BMI can mean different health risks.

What if a woman is athletic or muscular?

BMI can overestimate body fat in female athletes and muscular women. A woman with a BMI of 26 and low body fat may be healthier than someone with a BMI of 22 and high body fat. Body fat percentage, waist circumference, and how clothes fit are better indicators.

What BMI is considered obese for women?

A BMI of 30 or above is classified as obese for women, same as for men. This is divided into Class I (30–34.9), Class II (35–39.9), and Class III (40+).

What is the ideal BMI for fertility?

Research suggests the optimal BMI for fertility is roughly 20–24. Both underweight (below 18.5) and overweight (above 25) can affect ovulation and hormone balance.

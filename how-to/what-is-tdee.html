📊 What Is TDEE? Complete Guide

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read · Includes formula and calculator

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the total number of calories your body burns in a day. It's the sum of your basal metabolic rate (BMR), physical activity, and the energy used to digest food. Eating at your TDEE maintains your current weight; eating below it leads to weight loss; eating above it leads to weight gain.

This guide explains what TDEE is, how it differs from BMR, how to calculate it, and how to use it to hit your weight goals.

Definition
TDEE = Total Daily Energy Expenditure
The total number of calories you burn per day, combining your resting metabolism, all physical activity, and the energy required to digest food.

What Makes Up Your TDEE?

Your TDEE has four components. Together, they add up to the total calories you burn each day:

BMR — Basal Metabolic Rate
Calories burned at complete rest — breathing, circulation, cell repair
60–70%
TEA — Thermic Effect of Activity
Exercise and deliberate movement (gym, sports, running)
15–30%
NEAT — Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis
Small movements — walking, fidgeting, standing, chores
5–15%
TEF — Thermic Effect of Food
Energy used to digest and absorb what you eat
8–10%

Together, these four components make up your TDEE — the number you'd want to eat at to stay exactly the same weight.

TDEE vs BMR — What's the Difference?

This is the most common point of confusion. Here's the clear difference:

Aspect BMR TDEE
Stands forBasal Metabolic RateTotal Daily Energy Expenditure
What it measuresCalories burned at restCalories burned all day
Includes exercise?NoYes
Typical value1,200–1,800 cal/day1,800–3,000+ cal/day
Use it forBaseline calculationSetting calorie targets

Simple relationship: TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier. Your BMR is the foundation; your TDEE is the full-day total.

How to Calculate Your TDEE

You need two steps: calculate BMR first, then multiply by an activity factor.

Step 1 — Calculate BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor)

BMR Formula
Men: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

Step 2 — Multiply by Activity Factor

Activity Level Description Multiplier
SedentaryLittle/no exercise, desk job1.2
Lightly ActiveLight exercise 1–3 days/week1.375
Moderately ActiveModerate exercise 3–5 days/week1.55
Very ActiveHard exercise 6–7 days/week1.725
Extra ActiveVery hard exercise + physical job1.9

Complete Example

A 32-year-old man weighs 78 kg, is 180 cm tall, and exercises 4 days per week.

Worked Example
Step 1 — BMR: (10 × 78) + (6.25 × 180) − (5 × 32) + 5
BMR = 780 + 1125 − 160 + 5
BMR = 1,750 calories/day
Step 2 — TDEE: 1,750 × 1.55 (moderately active)
→ TDEE ≈ 2,713 calories/day

To maintain weight: eat ~2,713 cal/day. To lose 1 lb per week: eat ~2,213 cal/day. To gain 1 lb per week: eat ~3,213 cal/day.

How to Use TDEE for Your Goals

Maintenance: Eat exactly at your TDEE. Weight stays stable.

Weight loss: Eat 250–500 cal below your TDEE to lose 0.5–1 lb per week. A 500-calorie daily deficit produces ~1 lb loss/week.

Weight gain: Eat 250–500 cal above your TDEE to gain 0.5–1 lb per week. Aim for protein 0.7–1 g per pound of body weight to build muscle rather than fat.

Safety floor: Never eat below 1,200 calories/day (women) or 1,500 calories/day (men) without medical supervision. Going lower risks muscle loss, nutrient deficiencies, and metabolic slowdown.

What Affects Your TDEE?

Your TDEE isn't fixed. It changes based on:

  • Muscle mass: More muscle = higher BMR. Two people the same weight can have TDEEs that differ by 300+ calories.
  • Age: TDEE drops roughly 100–150 calories per decade after age 20 due to muscle loss.
  • Sex: Men typically have higher TDEEs due to greater average muscle mass and body size.
  • Genetics: Some people naturally burn more or less at rest.
  • NEAT: Small movements add up — some people burn 500+ extra calories a day just from fidgeting and walking.
  • Sleep: Chronic poor sleep can reduce TDEE and increase hunger hormones.
  • Hormones: Thyroid conditions, stress, and hormonal changes shift metabolism.
  • Diet quality: Very low-calorie diets cause metabolic adaptation — the body burns fewer calories to defend against starvation.

Why Your Calculated TDEE Might Be Off

Online TDEE calculators estimate within 5–10% for most people. But your actual TDEE can differ because:

  • Your activity multiplier may not perfectly match your lifestyle
  • Your NEAT may be higher or lower than the average person
  • Your metabolism may be faster or slower than the formula assumes
  • Your muscle mass may be above or below average for your weight

The best way to find your real TDEE: eat at your calculated number for 2 weeks, weigh yourself regularly, and adjust based on results. If your weight stayed the same, that's your actual TDEE.

Common TDEE Mistakes

Mistake 1: Using the wrong activity multiplier

Most people overestimate their activity. "Moderately active" (1.55) requires 3–5 days of real exercise per week. If you work a desk job and exercise twice a week, you're "lightly active" (1.375), not moderate.

Mistake 2: Treating TDEE as fixed

Your TDEE drops as you lose weight. A person who starts at 100 kg has a higher TDEE than the same person at 80 kg. Recalculate every 5–10 kg of weight change.

Mistake 3: Assuming the calculator is exact

TDEE is an estimate. Treat the calculator's number as a starting point, then adjust by observing real-world results over 2 weeks.

Mistake 4: Eating exercise calories back on top of TDEE

If you already set your activity multiplier to "moderately active," don't also add exercise calories. TDEE already includes them.

Mistake 5: Not tracking food accurately

People underestimate calorie intake by 20–30% on average. Weigh your food, log it honestly, and check your actual intake against your target for at least a week.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does TDEE stand for?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It's the total number of calories your body burns in a day, including BMR, physical activity, and the energy used to digest food.

What's the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is the calories your body burns at complete rest just to stay alive. TDEE is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor — it includes everything you do during the day, from walking to exercising.

How do I calculate my TDEE?

First calculate BMR using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, then multiply by an activity factor: sedentary 1.2, light 1.375, moderate 1.55, active 1.725, or very active 1.9.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight based on TDEE?

Eat 250–500 calories below your TDEE to lose 0.5–1 pound per week. A 500 calorie daily deficit produces about 1 pound of loss per week. Never go below 1,200 calories a day without medical supervision.

Is TDEE the same every day?

No. Your TDEE changes day to day based on activity, sleep, stress, and what you eat. It also declines with age as muscle mass decreases. It's an estimate, not a fixed number.

How accurate is a TDEE calculator?

TDEE calculators estimate within 5–10% for most people. Individual metabolism, muscle mass, genetics, and NEAT can shift the real number up or down. Track your weight for 2 weeks to fine-tune it.

Should I eat my TDEE calories or fewer to lose weight?

Fewer. To lose weight, eat 250–500 calories below your TDEE. Eating exactly at TDEE maintains weight. Eating above it gains weight.

Does TDEE include exercise calories?

Yes, if you set your activity multiplier accurately. TDEE already accounts for your typical weekly activity. Don't add "burned" calories on top of your TDEE target unless your exercise was unusual.
